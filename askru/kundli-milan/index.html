Kundli Milan: 36 guna, counted — and the reason for every one

Guna milan is arithmetic before it is advice. Ask Ru works all eight kootas from both birth charts, shows what each one scored and why, and says plainly where the classical sources disagree with each other.

What guna milan actually counts

Ashtakoota melaap compares two charts across eight factors — the kootas — each worth a fixed number of points. They are not equal: nadi alone is worth eight, varna one. Added up they come to thirty-six, which is where “36 guna” comes from.

KootaOut ofWhat it compares
Varna1The broad temperament of each Moon sign
Vashya2Which sign naturally yields to which
Tara3The count between the two birth nakshatras
Yoni4The animal nature assigned to each nakshatra
Graha Maitri5Whether the two Moon-sign lords are friends
Gana6Deva, manushya or rakshasa nature
Bhakoot7The distance between the two Moon signs
Nadi8The nadi of each birth nakshatra

Ask Ru computes every one of the eight from the two sidereal Moon positions, and prints the sentence behind the score — not only the number. A score you cannot interrogate is a number somebody has asked you to trust.

The order of the two people changes the answer

This surprises people, and it is correct: varna and gana are not symmetric. Swap who is entered as bride and who as groom and those two can score differently, so the total moves. That is why the app asks which chart is which rather than treating it as two anonymous charts.

A dosha is not a verdict

“You have Mangal dosha” is a sentence that lands on somebody thinking about a marriage, and the classical literature is full of conditions that cancel it. Ask Ru shows the three that guna milan raises — Bhakoot, Nadi and Gana dosha — each with the conditions found in the two charts that cancel it. The rule it applies is the plain one: any single recognised cancellation cancels the dosha, and the app says which one it found.

What it will not do. It will not tell you not to marry someone. It reports what the two charts contain, with the reason attached, and leaves the decision where it belongs.

Where the sources disagree, the app says so

Four of the eight kootas rest on reference tables that published authorities genuinely contradict each other on — the same pair of charts can score differently in two respectable books. Rather than pick one silently, Ask Ru marks those scores as disputed and tells you the score depends on which table is used.

That honesty costs something. Where a table has an unsettled middle case, the app takes the cautious reading, which can score a genuinely friendly pair one guna low. We would rather be a guna pessimistic and say why than confident and quiet.

Why a total out of 36 is not the whole answer

A number out of thirty-six is a summary, and summaries lose things. Two charts can reach the same total by different routes — strong nadi and weak bhakoot, or the reverse — and those are not the same situation. That is the reason the app shows all eight lines rather than one figure, and the reason it reads the result to you in sentences rather than handing over a verdict.

Questions people ask

How many guna are needed for marriage?

There is no number in the classical texts that makes a marriage valid or invalid; the figure people quote varies by tradition and by family. Ask Ru shows the eight scores and what each one means, and leaves the judgement to you and whoever you trust with it.

Is kundli milan the same as guna milan?

In common use, yes — both describe comparing two birth charts before marriage. Ashtakoota melaap is the specific eight-factor method this page describes, and it is what Ask Ru computes.

Does Ask Ru need an exact birth time for both people?

Guna milan is worked from the Moon, so it needs each person's birth date, time and place well enough to place the Moon in the right nakshatra. Where a birth time is uncertain, the app says what it cannot settle instead of choosing for you.

Is Ru a real astrologer?

No. Ru is an AI, and the app says so on the screen of every reading. She is a companion rather than a human astrologer, and she is not a therapist.
